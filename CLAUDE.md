# Lumipals: project guide

Lumipals is a light-bending puzzle game: rotate mirrors and crystals to steer beams of light so every gem lights up. It is for all ages and is published at https://lumipals.app/play/ (GitHub Pages with a custom domain; the domain is at Cloudflare, DNS only; the old https://ringh0st.github.io/prism/ redirects there). The game is at `/play/`, its own page at `/lumipals/`, and `/` is the page for all the owner's games (below). The store name is "Rinny's Lumipals: Light Puzzle". It was called Prism before; the repository and `prism.*` storage keys keep that name on purpose, so players keep their progress.

## Rinny's Games: the front page (site/index.html)

https://lumipals.app/ is the page for all the owner's games, under the name she chose, **Rinny's Games** (also the studio name on both games' opening screens, `STUDIO_NAME`). It is a plain HTML page in the "featured + all games" layout she picked: one game is the star at the top (a swipeable slide per game; the page opens on a different one each week), all games are tiles under it (Lumipals, Slime Merge at slime.lumipals.app, Marbles at `/marbles/`, a "Coming soon" tile). Warm cream, so each game's own colors stand out; on a wide screen the star is a tilted phone on the game's color. It keeps the Google verification tag and the Privacy and Terms links (the payment provider looks at this address). Lumipals' own page (how to play, accounts) is at `/lumipals/` (`site/lumipals/index.html`); the game stays at `/play/`. It can be put on a phone's Home Screen (`site/manifest.webmanifest`, its own "R" icon in `site/games/`, and an "Add to Home Screen" chip that opens the steps for iPhone and Android). Every game has a small four-squares icon back to this page (Lumipals: at the start of the map's top row, `map.allGames`, not in the iPhone app; Slime Merge: in its top bar on its home page). To add a game: one `.star` slide, one `.tile`, one screenshot (640×1280 JPG) in `site/games/`.

## Marbles (marbles/): a second game in this repository

The owner's schoolyard marbles game ("גולות"), published at https://lumipals.app/marbles/. It is its own small Vite app (`marbles/`, `npm run marbles` to develop, built into `dist/marbles` by the deploy workflow, its version in `marbles/version.json`); none of Lumipals' rules about colors, i18n or CSS modules apply to it (plain `styles.css`, English, warm sand and chalk). The owner asked for the real complexity and feel of marbles, with sounds, and allowed three.js.

- **Physics** (`src/game/physics.js`, pure and deterministic, fixed steps): marbles of different size and weight (glass targets; shooters: Glass, heavy Steelie, big Jumbo), rolling friction, elastic knocks, pebbles and a kerb that bounce, sand that slows, and the hole ("mor"): a slow marble drops in, a fast one skips over its lip.
- **Grounds** (`GROUNDS` in physics.js, picked on the home page like Slime Merge's jars; owner: the game was too easy and should feel more real): packed earth, beach sand (marbles stop fast), asphalt (fast and bouncy), grass (bumpy: shots curve) and the slope (everything rolls back downhill). Each has its own drag, kerb bounce, slope and bumps, its own picture (`groundPicture` in view3d.js) and rolling sound. Also for difficulty: a flick strays a little, a hard one most (`strayOf`; the fan round the aim shows how far), a shooter left inside the ring can be knocked out for one of its side's marbles, and the Mor has a lip fast marbles skip.
- **Made hard on purpose** (owner, twice: "too easy", "not challenging"). Measuring showed why: whoever shot first ran the table from inside the pack, and a thoughtless full-power shot beat the computer most of the time. A careless player (always full power at the nearest marble) must lose to Normal and Hard; check with a script like that before easing anything. What makes it so: in the **Ring** both sides' six marbles lie mixed (warm colors are side `you`, cool are `foe`, `team` on the marble), you win the other side's and knocking out your own gives it to them, first to 4; a shooter stays where it stopped and shoots from there (sending it back to its line after every shot was tried and undone: the owner couldn't score at all on sand), and one left inside the ring can be knocked out; who starts is drawn; the ground grips (`drag`), so only a hard, true hit knocks a marble out, and hard flicks stray most; in the **Chase** the shooters start apart among six pebbles and sand, a hit shooter goes back to its line, and a wild shot into the kerb is "out" and gives the other side a marble; the computer picks shots that still work if they stray (`pickShot` tries each a little to both sides) and its own flicks stray less on harder levels. The challenges are checked with a perfect hand (`LEVELS.sure`) on a smoother yard (`SWEPT_EARTH`): possible, but a real flick strays.
- **Yards with shapes** (owner's pick from a list of difficulty ideas; `terrain`, `puddles`, `features` on the world, laid out from the seed in `newGame`, so no two yards are alike and both phones of a live game agree): one or two of a **mound** (rolls marbles off; a light bump), a **dip** (pulls them in; a dark hollow), a **puddle** (stops them dead) and **gates** (two posts in front of each line; "Sweep!" doesn't remove them). And the ring isn't always a circle (her brother remembered triangles): `ring.shape` is a circle, a square or a sideways triangle (the same for both ends), judged by `depthInRing`. The line at the start of a game says what this yard has. Challenges stay plain (`terrain: false`).
- **Fair turns** (owner: "once someone scores he has another go, so the first person is likely to win; we need something balanced"; `endTurn`, `BONUS_SHOTS` in rules.js): a good shot earns one bonus shot, never a run; and the game is only decided when both sides have had the same number of turns, so the side that shot second always gets a turn to answer a side that reached the target first. Level after a round, they play on until one leads; with nothing left to shoot at and level bags it is a draw.
- **Every point explained** (owner: "I don't understand the logic … suddenly he wins"; `story.js`): after each shot one line says what happened and who got the marble (`tellShot`), a "+1" jumps up from that side's bag, the line under the bags always says whose turn, bonus shot or "last turn: catch up" (`turnStatus`), a hit shooter glides back to its line instead of jumping, the computer waits 1.6 s so the line can be read, and the result card says how the last shot ended it.
- **Rules** (`rules.js`), from the Israeli rules the owner sent: three games for two sides. **Ring** (knock marbles out of the chalk circle), **Mor** (the hole: push them into it; your own shooter in it is a foul and gives a marble back), **Chase** (hit the other shooter). A good shot gives another turn, a miss passes it. Two calls, once a game each, from the Israeli "bakol" and "bagova" (the code keeps those names): **"Sweep!"** clears the pebbles off, **"High shot!"** flies over everything at first. Everything a player reads is in English letters (owner: "it's for everyone"), with one word kept on purpose because she likes it: the hole is called the **Mor** (the game is "Mor", and How to play says it is the little hole in the middle).
- **Computer** (`ai.js`): tries shots ahead with the real physics (aiming a marble at the hole or out of the ring) and plays the best with a shakier hand on Easy. **Challenges** (`challenges.js`): 12 yards for one player with a few shots and stars; a test checks each can be won.
- **View** (`view3d.js`, three.js): glass marbles that really roll, shadows, the yard at a slight tilt; taps are cast onto the ground. **Sounds** (`audio.js`, made in code): glass clack by force, pebble, kerb, the plop into the hole, rolling hiss.
- **How to play** (`components/HowTo.jsx`): a sheet for newcomers (how to shoot, the three games, whose turn, the calls, good to know); it opens by itself on a first visit and from the "?" at the top or the chip on the home page.
- Playing: pull back anywhere and let go (`components/Yard.jsx`). Against the computer (3 levels), two players on one phone, the challenges, or a friend live.
- **Live with a code** (`live.js`, the same Supabase Realtime broadcast as Slime Merge's fights, nothing stored; owner: "like the slime, but one board", with a 10-second turn shown as a bar): one player starts a game (their game and ground) and shares a five-digit code, the other joins. One yard: the host is side `you`, the friend is `foe` and sees the yard from its far end (`flip` in view3d.js), so each shoots from the bottom of their own screen. Every shot is sent and played on both phones, and the shooter's phone then sends where everything ended (`snapshotOf`/`applySnapshot`), so the yards can't drift apart. A turn not used in `TURN_SECONDS` passes. `?local` on the dev address links two tabs without the server. Not yet tried on two real phones.
- Still to do: more challenges, night mode, grounds in the challenges.

## Product requirements (from the owner, apply to all work)

- **Best UX/UI:** polished, delightful, minimal and fun. Every interaction gets clear, juicy feedback.
- **Colors:** warm, relaxing and calm (owner feedback: bright yellow and red felt too fiery). **Purple and blue must not be the main colors.** Blue may appear only as a small in-game color (for example one beam color among several), never as the brand, UI or background color.
- **Addictive, dopamine-rich loop:** satisfying solve moments, stars, streaks, rewards, unlocks and progress you can see.
- **Levels with a difficulty rhythm:** mostly approachable levels, with a **Hard** level now and then and an occasional **Extra Hard** challenge level. These are clearly marked and give bigger rewards.
- **Challenging and game-like, not a web page:** 1000+ levels (50 worlds × 20, `npm run levels`), move limits, an adventure map, chunky outlined game UI, a mascot (Lumi) with funny lines, and surprises (disco/night/boss levels, chests).
- **Rewards:** 1–3 stars per level based on moves, world completion rewards, a daily puzzle with a streak, and cosmetic unlocks.
- **Accounts:** play as a guest immediately with local progress. Signing in with Google or Apple is optional and syncs progress, using Supabase. The Apple button always shows in the iPhone app and appears on the web once the Apple provider is enabled in Supabase (`src/lib/authProviders.js`).
- **Admin:** only the owner can use a level editor and publish levels, and has a testing switch in Me → account that opens every level on that device without writing fake progress (`src/lib/openAllLevels.js`). Levels are data in the database, so new levels appear without an app update. Its Stats tab shows anonymous play counts (`supabase/play_stats.sql`, `usePlayStats`, `src/game/playCounts.js`): tries, win rate, out-of-moves and gave-up per level, guests included, no personal data, and a Walls list of levels most players fail.
- **Responsive and accessible:** phone-first, and works on tablet and desktop too. Full keyboard support, screen-reader labels, visible focus, good color contrast, reduced-motion support, and color is never the only signal (use shapes and symbols too).
- **Code quality:** best practices, reusable components and modules, consistent naming, small focused files, no dead code.

## Economy (see src/game/catalog.js and supabase/economy.sql)

- **Sparks** are earned by playing. **Diamonds** are premium: earned by finishing a world, 3-starring a world or every 7-day streak, or bought with real money on the website (Stripe Managed Payments as merchant of record; Lemon Squeezy until it closes on 31 March 2027; the `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER` secret picks one, see PAYMENTS.md). In the iPhone app the same packs are Apple in-app purchases (Apple's rule for using web-bought currency in an app; `src/lib/applePurchases.js` with the `@capgo/native-purchases` StoreKit plugin, `APPLE_PURCHASES_ENABLED`): the app sends only the transaction id, `supabase/functions/apple-purchase` asks Apple's App Store Server API what was bought and credits the buyer (`appAccountToken` = the account id) through the same `grant_purchase`, `apple-notifications` takes diamonds back on a refund, and a paid-but-undelivered purchase is delivered at the next start; a test keeps the product ids in step. Not yet tried on a real device. Diamonds always require an account.
- **Helpers:** Hint, Lens, Auto-turn and Skip. They are capped so buying isn't a shortcut through the game: max 5 held per helper, 3 helpers bought with sparks per day, and star caps (Hint/Lens → 2★, Auto-turn/Skip → 1★). Sparks can never become diamonds.
- For signed-in players the **server is the source of truth**: every currency change goes through a `security definer` function in `supabase/economy.sql`, never a direct table write. Prices exist in both `catalog.js` and `economy.sql`; a test keeps them in sync.
- **Boosters** (`BOOSTERS` in catalog.js, `useBoosters`, `BoosterPicker`): three pre-level boosters picked on the level card (main road from World 2; not in the daily, events, Rush or challenges) and spent at Let's go: **Spare Moves** (+3 to the move limit, stars unchanged), **Undo** (`steadyHand`: take back up to 2 taps; the tap still counts for stars but the move comes back; the button sits in the header) and **Head Start** (Lumi turns one mirror of the best solution before the start, not a move, ★★ at most, kept through restarts). Held like helpers (same `helpers` inventory, cap 5, server `use_helper`), bought only with sparks (60) inside the 3-a-day limit, never diamonds. They also come from calendar days 3 and 5 and from presents' helper surprise. Touching a booster keeps the card up until Let's go.
- **Win streak** (src/game/winStreak.js): main-road levels won in a row (`progress.winStreak`, on this device). From 3 in a row every level starts with a free Spare Moves, from 6 also a free Undo (shown on the level card with a flame and the streak; nothing is taken from the inventory). Running out of moves ends it; leaving, restarting or a Skip doesn't.
- **Level numbers and milestones** (`levelNumber` in worlds.js, src/game/milestones.js): built-in levels are numbered 1–1000 on the map, PLAY and titles ("Level 148", the world as the subtitle); ids stay `w8-08`. Solving the 50th, 100th, 250th, 500th and 1000th level throws a party on the win screen (trophy with the number, big celebration).
- **Pals** (profile characters, src/game/pals.js and supabase/pals.sql): 48 Pals, including 8 Legendary and 10 Mythic ones from myths and legends (phoenix, qilin, griffin…). Every Pal is a hand-drawn kawaii sticker with one SVG per form (`src/assets/pals/drawn/<id>-<1…5>.svg`, Baby to Master, drawn by `PalArt`) on a shared 100×100 stage: head top y≈25, eyes (39,51)/(61,51), neck y≈70, floor y≈94, so every outfit piece fits every Pal and form (capes and cloaks, `Cloak`, hang behind it with a collar and clasp in front; worn items are drawn like the Pals, a thin line in a deeper shade of their own colors plus a soft shadow (`ItemOutline` in PalArt), so they stand out on Pals of the same color; hats sit deep on the head, brim on the forehead (`HAT_DEPTH`), fitted to each drawing, widened to its forehead and lowered onto heads that sit low, from `palOutfitFit.json`, which `npm run pals:fit` measures). Each form is drawn older: signature features grow (tail, mane, wings, antlers, shell), Champ adds a small new detail and Master a crown-like feature of its own with a golden glow; every form is shown at the same size (growing up shows in how it looks, never in size: each drawing has its own scale in `palFit.json`; run `npm run pals:fit` after adding or redrawing Pals). From a player's feedback: a Pal must read as its animal at a glance (Waffles the puppy was redrawn with short floppy ears, a patch over one eye, eyebrow dots, a spot on its side and a wagging tail; its long ears had looked like hair), and stray decorative strokes on a face read as wrinkles (two chin lines were taken off Maple the red panda), so keep faces clean. More from players and the owner: the frog was drawn fresh after two misses (bumps on top read as ears, then pale rings round the eyes read as goggles): a wide flat head and two eye domes drawn as one shape with one outline (stroked, then filled again on top) and one gradient over the whole picture (`userSpaceOnUse`, so no seam), big eyes in the domes, a wide smile, folded back legs with long webbed feet and webbed hands; the owner liked the elephant redrawn the same way; the unicorn sits like the other Pals on haunches with two front legs and pink hooves (its side-on legs looked wrong), the giant panda has a smooth round head and a plain tummy, and Dozy the dream-eater is drawn as a sleepy little elephant that sits on its legs, with big ears, a long curled trunk and tusks (a baku has an elephant's trunk; the owner found the old lying-down shape too fat and not like an elephant). The first Pal turns up as a surprise reward after 5 solved levels ("You found a friend!", `src/game/firstFriend.js`, `FriendFoundDialog`), so the game reads as a puzzle game first; unlock the rest by playing. Only the partner Pal grows: every solved level (not a Skip) adds one, and 20 grow it into its next form (Baby, Junior, Buddy, Champ, Master); each Pal keeps its own count. Evolution can never be bought; sparks only buy cosmetic accessories. **Pal powers** (src/game/palPowers.js, `PalPowerBadge`, `PalPowerChip`): while it's the partner, every Pal helps in main-road levels with one power (`power` in pals.js): extra moves, take-backs or free hints. It grows with the form (Baby/Junior 1, Buddy/Champ 2, Master 3; Legendary and Mythic start one step stronger) and never lowers the ★★★ target (moves and take-backs only add to the limit; a free hint caps at ★★ like any hint). Not in the daily, events, Rush or challenges. It's shown as a coin with the amount on Pal cards, team tiles, the forms strip, the partner hero, the first-friend card and the level card (playtesters didn't know what Pals were for). The Pals screen's Collection tab (`PalTeam`) lists every Pal; a locked one shows its goal as a lock, a picture and a number (★ stars, a map for worlds, a flame for a daily-puzzle streak, a gift for Lucky), and tapping that badge says it in words (`LockHint`; a player asked what the lock and flame meant); "Play with" (`TeamSwitcher`, `playablePals`) holds every owned or unlocked Pal (an unlocked one joins the moment it's picked, no separate "add" step), and the level card's Pal chip opens the same switcher, so the partner (and its power) can be changed right before a level. **How Pals work** (`PalGuide`): one picture loop (win → it grows, bigger → stronger help, 10 ★ → a present, more Pals join and you pick who plays), opened by the ? on the partner hero and by itself on the first Pals visit when no other Pal notice is up; in levels a first-time spotlight points at the helping Pal (`pal` in levelTips.js), and the win screen shows the stronger power when a Pal grows.
- **The Grove** (src/game/grove.js, supabase/grove.sql): the Pals' home in the enchanted forest (Pals → Grove → Decorate); only the partner Pal is shown there. 22 decor items in 7 spots (backdrop, home, seat, light, garden, water, sky), bought with sparks and placed one per spot; a **background** (`backdrop`: Blossom hills, Snowy pines, Desert oasis, Sunny cove, `groveBackdrops.jsx`) swaps the forest for its own land under the same time-of-day sky (no falling leaves in the desert or at the beach). The Pal there is alive (`src/game/groveLife.js`, `usePalLife`): each Pal there (`GrovePal`) wanders, plays (jump, spin, dance…), uses placed decor (sits on the seat, splashes in the water…), follows the player's time of day (dawn/day/dusk/night sky) and naps 23:00–06:00; tapping pets it. The forest changes with the seasons (`seasonOf`: snow, petals, autumn leaves), one of your other Pals visits most daytimes (`visitingFriend`), and finishing a world throws a Grove party once (`useGroveParty`). After each win the partner cheers (`src/game/palCheers.js`). Once a day the partner says hello on the map beside PLAY (`palGreetings.js`, `PalHello`): news first (almost grown, event weekend, streak), otherwise a time-of-day hello; it waits for the splash (`useIsSplashShowing`).
- **Gifts** (src/game/gifts.js, supabase/gifts.sql, `src/components/gifts/`): every gift opens the same way (`GiftReveal`: a wrapped box or chest waits for a tap, wobbles, pops open, and the things inside come out one by one with a chime; a tap shows everything; calm for reduced motion). The daily gift is a **7-day gift calendar** (`GIFT_CALENDAR`, `GiftCalendar`): it grows through the week, day 7 is the big gift (a diamond for accounts, a Skip for guests), and a missed day pauses it (it never resets). The partner Pal brings a **present** for every 10 ★ from built-in levels (`PresentButton` on the map bar with a filling meter, `PalPresent` beside PLAY holding it, `PresentNote` on the win screen): 20 sparks plus one surprise with its odds shown (`PresentOdds`: helper 45%, a bag of 40 more sparks 30%, an outfit piece or Grove decor you don't own 25%), drawn on the server for accounts (`claim_present`). Inspired by Brawl Stars' Starr Drops (research: the surprise reveal brought its players back), each present also has a rarity decided when it's drawn and revealed as it opens: it glows up step by step (`GiftReveal` glow steps, color plus a symbol ◆ ★ ✦, `sfx.glowUp`): Plain 65%, Rare 23% (+20 sparks), Epic 9% (+40, a sure item), Legendary 3% (+80, a sure item, a diamond for accounts or 40 sparks for guests) (`PRESENT_RARITIES`, odds shown in `PresentOdds`). No dud results: the plainest present still holds sparks plus a helper, a sparks bag or an item. Several waiting presents open together in one window, one after another, then a summary (`GiftReveal` `count`); each present charges up first (a bar fills notch by notch ◆ ★ ✦, glowing up at each notch its rarity reaches), and a tap hurries it along. Presents start once the first Pal is found. **Win gifts** (`WIN_GIFTS`, `WinGiftLadder` on the win screen, `claim_win_gift`): the 1st, 3rd and 6th different level won each day (not a Skip) bring a small gift (10 sparks; 15 + Hint; 25 + Lens); the server counts its own results since the player's midnight; a day without play costs nothing. Run gifts.sql after rush.sql (and again after re-running social.sql, pals.sql or grove.sql).
- **Pal records** (src/game/palRecords.js, `PalRecords`, `PalRecordNote`): five medals per Pal earned together while it's the partner (5 Hard wins, ★★★ in 10 levels, 3 gems in one tap, a boss with ★★★, growing into a Master). A row of medals with filling rings on the Pal's card (words only on tap) and a shining medal on the win screen. Pride only: they pay nothing, can't be bought, and are counted on this device.
- **Show, don't tell** (owner feedback, like Brawl Stars): rarity and rewards read from color, light, size, symbols and sound, not words. The present's rarity is a color, a ring of light and a big symbol (words only for screen readers); the odds are pictures with percentages. Prefer icons and motion over sentences in new UI.
- **Nicknames** (src/game/nicknames.js, supabase/nicknames.sql): players type their Top-board name or roll a random one. The same rules run in the game and on the server: 3–16 letters/numbers/spaces, no long digit runs, and a blocked-word list (kept in sync by a test).
- **Titles and player level** (src/game/titles.js): titles are earned by stars, each with its own color via TitleBadge, which also shows the player's Glow (★ and a number) (`playerLevelFor`: Lv 2 at 5 ★, rising slower later, Lv 35 at 2975 ★). The Me screen shows the level bar and the Title road (`TitleRoad`); the win screen shows Level up!/New title! (`climbFor`, `LevelUp`).
- **Player cards** (supabase/player_cards.sql, `PlayerCardDialog`): tap anyone on the Top board to see their partner Pal, title, weekly points, stars, worlds and Pals collected, each next to yours. Looked up by board place (no account ids are exposed). The Me screen shows Pals collected too.
- **Rarity and the Shop:** Pals are Basic, ★ Legendary (8, only from Lucky gifts or the Shop) or ✦ Mythic (10). The Shop shows 6 Basic Pals a week per player (`weeklyAdoptShelf`/`_weekly_basics`, new ones every Monday UTC, owned ones stay marked) to adopt by choice (20💎); each day every player gets one random Legendary (40💎) and one random Mythic (60💎) pick, always shown before buying, and can adopt at most 2 Legendary/Mythic Pals a week (`RARE_ADOPTS_PER_WEEK`, enforced in `adopt_pal`). In Hebrew the rarities are רגיל, אגדי and מיתולוגי. **Lucky Pal gift:** every 200 solved levels a free random Pal you don't have (70% Basic, 20% Legendary, 10% Mythic, odds shown), drawn on the server for accounts (`claim_lucky_pal`).
- **Weekend events** (src/game/events.js, supabase/events.sql, `npm run events`): every Saturday and Sunday 10 themed puzzles (themes take turns: Mirror Madness, Portal Party…), made ahead of time in src/data/events/ (one file per weekend, a year ahead; run `npm run events` to add more). Open after World 1; no Skip; each solve grows the partner Pal. Finishing all 10 pays 150 sparks and that event's stamp (collected on the event page).
- **Light Rush** (src/game/rush.js, supabase/rush.sql, `src/screens/rush/`, `useRushRun`): a weekly run (Monday, UTC) of 30 built-in puzzles, two from each of Worlds 1–15, the same for everyone. Opens after World 3. One pot of 12 spare moves for the whole run: solving in par costs nothing, extra moves and restarts spend spares (the run is saved on every move, so leaving mid-puzzle counts as a restart), +3 every 5 puzzles; running out ends the run. No helpers. Milestones (5/10/20/30 puzzles) pay sparks once a week; a test keeps them in sync with rush.sql. Weekly board of best runs under nicknames (`rush_board`).
- **Pal quests** (src/game/quests.js, supabase/quests.sql): each week (Monday, UTC) the partner Pal asks for three goals (solve levels, ★★★ levels, Hard levels; the bigger or smaller of each takes turns), counted once per level per week and paid in sparks. The server counts from its own level results; a test keeps the quest list in sync.
- **Daily missions** (src/game/dailyMissions.js, supabase/daily_missions.sql, `DailyMissions`, `MissionsButton`): three small goals a day from the player's local midnight (like win gifts): one "solve N" plus two of other kinds (★★★, Hard, daily puzzle, Glowbug rescue; guests also combo and present), drawn the same way for everyone from the missions the player can already do (`minSolved`, from 3 solved levels) and kept once the day starts. 10–20 sparks each, +10 with the last of the three. Shown as "Today" rings above the Pal quests (Pals → Quests); a tick on the map bar while one waits. Collecting a mission or a quest opens no pop-up (owner's ask): the sparks fly from the button straight into the counter at the top, which counts up (`flyRewardsToCounter` in `src/lib/rewardFlight.js`). Use that for any plain sparks payout; keep `GiftReveal` for real gifts with a surprise inside. Accounts: the server keeps the day's three and counts from its own results (`my_daily_missions`, `claim_daily_mission`; guests-only missions are left out); a test keeps both in sync. Run daily_missions.sql after gifts.sql and glowbugs.sql.
- **Weekly theme collections** (src/game/themes.js, supabase/themed_shop.sql): each week (Monday, UTC) the Shop's Pal section opens on "This week": one theme's outfit pieces and Grove decor (Elven Woods, Autumn Harvest, Winter Wonderland, Pirate Cove take turns), some for sparks and the showpieces for diamonds. Every collection comes back later in the year (no "now or never"). Art: `themeOutfitArt.jsx`, `themeDecorShapes.jsx`. Re-run themed_shop.sql after re-running pals.sql or grove.sql (it updates wear/place for theme items).
- **Try it on** (`TryOnDialog`): tapping any outfit piece or decoration in the Shop shows it on your own Pal (or in your Grove, without the Pals so nothing hides it) first, then Buy → "Wear it now"/"Place it now". Short on sparks → Play; short on diamonds → the Diamonds tab (ask-a-parent note; earn them in the app); diamonds need an account.
- All-ages fairness: **no paid loot boxes**: anything bought is shown before paying. Random rewards are only free, earned gifts with their odds shown. No fake timers or pressure tactics, clear prices, and an "ask a parent" note wherever money is involved.

Style: "Sunlit Paper". Matte surfaces, warm layered shadows and buttons that pop up a little (owner: "everything is super flat"; a little 3D, not a lot): a soft highlight along the top and a solid darker edge underneath in the color's `-ledge` token, and a press pushes the button down onto its edge (`Button`, the map's level circles and PLAY, the map's top-row buttons, the helpers in a level). The owner then asked for the highlights to be way more noticeable, so the top highlight is bright (50% white, 3px) with a soft shade along the bottom and an edge of 5–8px; the flat board's tiles stand up the same way (`.plate` in Board.module.css: bright top edge, a darker edge underneath in the tile's own color), and a tile under the light is clearly lit (a strong honey fill and a glow round it, `.plateLit`). The owner then found chunky glossy game buttons (thick edge, gloss band, bounce) "too 3D, but in a not so nice way", and plain flat gradient pills not what she meant either; from an options sheet she picked **the sticker and the slim edge together**: `Button` is a pill with a flat face in one color, a 3px border in a deeper shade of that same color (never black), a white bold label with a small shadow in the deep shade, and a thin 4px edge of the deep shade underneath. No gloss, gradients or bounce. Its states read at a glance: hovered a little brighter, pressed down onto its edge, focused with a ring (keyboard only), disabled plain grey. `gold` is honey with a dark label, `secondary` a cream sticker with dark text. A variant sets `--face`, `--deep` and `--label`; never override a button's `background` or `border`. PLAY on the map is the same sticker; the map's top-row buttons, level circles and the Map tab carry the same slim edge (3–4px). No outlines, glass, neon glows or gradient text; only light beams glow. The focus ring is for keyboard players only (`src/lib/inputMode.js` remembers the last kind of input; a screen that focuses its main button by itself, like the "Let's go!" card, drew a red frame round it on phones). Each colored surface uses the `--action|reward|warm|alert|calm-*` tokens (fill, ledge, label) so text keeps WCAG contrast.

## Conventions

- React + Vite, plain CSS with design tokens (CSS custom properties) defined once and themed for light and dark.
- Components: `PascalCase.jsx`, one component per file, colocated `ComponentName.module.css`.
- Hooks: `useSomething.js`. Pure game logic (beam tracing, level validation, scoring) lives in `src/game/` with no React or DOM, so it can be unit tested.
- Names describe intent: `traceBeam`, `isLevelSolved`, `starsForMoves`, not `doStuff` or `helper`.
- Sound and haptics live in `src/audio/` (generated music-box tones, no files). Each moment has its own named sound (`sfx.outOfMoves`, `sfx.palGrow`…) and a haptic strength; keep failure sounds kind, never harsh. Inside a world, the level-start jingle, gem chimes and win fanfare use that world's voice (`src/audio/worldVoices.js`: scale, instrument, motif and a nature sound; set by `sfx.setWorldVoice`). iPhone haptics use the hidden-switch trick in `haptics.js`. Levels have quiet generated background music in the world's voice (`src/audio/music.js`: soft pad plus sparse wandering notes, never the same twice; fades out for the win, stops when the app is hidden), with its own Music switch in Settings. Sound effects and music share one context (`audioContext.js`).
- **Pieces and twists** (`src/game/pieces.js`): a new piece every world through world 17 (the last five are the darkness), taught one per level with a spotlight on the board, never two introductions in a row (a test walks worlds 1–15): mirrors 1-1, walls 1-4, fixed mirrors 1-10, splitters 2-1, color glass 3-1, shadow crystals 4-1, **twin mirrors** 5-1 (same mark = turn together; `link`), **portals** 6-1 (same pair = light jumps across; `pair`), **cracking mirrors** 7-1 (`fragile`: 3 turns, then fixed; hints treat cracked ones as locked), **fog** 8-1 (`fog` on gems/walls/shades: hidden until light touches the cell), **prism crystals** 9-1 (fixed `prism` tile: light splits out to both sides), **light gates** 10-1 (on the flat board the switch is an on/off toggle, grey with the knob on the left while dark and glowing with the knob on the right once lit; the gate is a wooden door with a padlock that stands open as two leaves at the sides; a dashed wire in the pair's color runs from each switch to its gate and lights up with running dashes when on, `GateWires` in TileArt.jsx; owner: "I like the idea of a switch, but it's hard to understand"; `gate` + `switch` with the same `pair`: the gate is a wall until light hits its switch; `traceBeams` retraces until the open gates settle; the first lamp's light feeds the switch, so gate levels have two lamps), **turning lamps** 11-1 (emitter with `turnable`: tap swings it a quarter turn clockwise and back, `lampDirection`), **one-sided mirrors** 12-1 (`oneSided: 0 | 1`, the silvered face; the dark back blocks light, `hitsSilveredFace`). Later worlds mix them in (`TWIST_WORLDS`/`TWIST_MIX` in worlds.js). **The darkness** (worlds 13–17, `src/game/darkness.js`): Smudge (כתמון), a cheeky shadow, steals the light, and his pieces race the player move by move, so the order of taps matters: **creeping shadows** 13-1 (`level.glooms: [{ trail: [cells], gem }]`: a cell per move toward the gem, light burns it where it stands or as it creeps into light, past its trail it swallows the gem), **fading gems** 14-1 (`fade: n`: light it within n moves or it goes dark), **dying lamps** 15-1 (`power`, `reach`: the light reaches `reach` + power left cells; out of power, the lamp goes out), **voids** 16-1 (fixed `void` tile: swallows light in its cell and the four next to it, in `traceBeams`) and **eclipses** 17-1 (`level.eclipse: 3`: every 3rd move the darkness moves twice). The game replays the standing taps (`replayDarkness` in `usePuzzle`, so an Undo rolls the darkness back), the board draws it (`DarknessLayer`, `darkTileArt.jsx`: Smudge gliding over his dotted trail, countdown badges, the void's halo, the eclipse chip), losing to it ends the level like running out of moves with its own headline (`out.dark.*`; in Light Rush it's a restart), and hints pick a tap in a safe order (`findSafeOrder`). The builder adds it on top of a finished board (`darknessBuilder.js`, `darknessFor`/`DARK_WORLDS`/`DARK_MIX` in worlds.js): voids where they cut off cheaper routes, then lamp power, fade deadlines and shadow trails tuned so a safe order exists but random orders often lose (`orderRisk`; gentler on normal levels, and the piece's first level gives plenty of room). From world 15 bosses bring Smudge's shadows (two from world 25) and show his face; Lumi introduces him on 13-1 (`intro.smudge`). Every new piece keeps two states per switch, so the solver still finds par exactly; the solver treats twins as one switch. Difficulty follows the level number 1–1000 (`generatorPresetFor`: taps needed rise from about 4 to 6, Hard +2 and bosses +3, with a turning-piece budget a few above the taps; tricky, not cluttered), so a new world never starts easier than the one before. The solver follows the light and only decides pieces the light reaches (`NeedsDecision` in beam.js), so boards can have many turning pieces (the budget keeps them to 6–16); solutions are `{ mask, care }` bit masks. After changing `generatorPresetFor`, run `npm run levels -- <worlds>`.

Night levels keep their dark floor (the scene's colors step aside, its pictures stay); disco levels keep the scene's floor. `?theme=pond` on the game's address shows one place on every level.
- **Beam style on every board** (`src/lib/beamStyle.js`): the chosen beam style (Me, `progress.beamTheme`, the CSS variable `--beam-sun`) colors plain sunlight on the flat board, the 3D board (`elements/beam.js`) and the themed boards, where it must show clearly (owner checked): the beam and its core take the color (`NaturalBeam`) and so does the glow of the tiles under it (`floorGlowColor`, passed to every theme's tile as `glow`); colored lights keep their own colors.
- **Level landscapes** (`src/screens/play/WorldBackdrop.jsx`, `backdrop/`): each level gets its own arrangement of the world's scenery plus two of the world's animals (`BIOME_CREATURES`: camels, lizards and tumbleweeds in the desert, ducks at the lagoon…), seeded by level id. Keep them small, slow and quiet; nothing moves for reduced motion.
- **Challenge links** (`src/game/challenges.js`, `ChallengeShare`, `ChallengeRoute`, `ChallengeResultDialog`): the win screen's "Challenge a friend" shares `lumipals.app/play/#/challenge/<level>/<moves>/<nickname>` (public nickname only, checked by the nickname rules). Anyone, even a new guest, plays that level in `mode="challenge"`: no helpers, nothing saved, then sees both scores and can challenge back. Everything is in the link; no database.
- **Solve replay** (`src/game/replay.js`, `src/lib/replay/`, `ReplayShare`): the win screen's "Share replay" records the winning taps as an 8–15 s vertical MP4 (canvas + MediaRecorder, drawn in the game's light style, ending with the stars and lumipals.app) and opens the share sheet, or downloads it. Hidden where the browser can't record.
- **Splash and version** (`src/components/splash/SplashScreen.jsx`, `src/lib/appInfo.js`): a 4-second opening screen with the game name, "A Rinny's Games production" and the version from `package.json` (bump it for each release). Tap, Enter or Escape skips it; pop-ups wait until it's gone.
- **iPhone app** (IOS.md): Capacitor wraps the built game (`ios/`, bundle id `app.lumipals`, `npm run ios`). Use `isNativeApp` from `src/lib/platform.js` for app-only behavior: no web checkout (diamonds are Apple in-app purchases there) or auto-update in the app, native haptics, sign-in via a Safari sheet returning to `app.lumipals://auth`, and Sign in with Apple.
- **Languages** (`src/i18n/`): English, Spanish and Hebrew. `useI18n()` gives `t(key, params, fallback)` (plurals via `count` and `key.one`/`key.other`, `{name}` placeholders, English fallback) and `line(key, englishLines)` for Lumi's quips. Dictionaries: `src/i18n/strings/{en,es,he}.js` (play, map, win) plus one file per area in `src/i18n/strings/sections/` (me, pals, shop, grove, extras; each exports en/es/he); English is the source, and tests check that every key the code uses exists and that es/he match en's keys and placeholders. Game content keeps its English in the game modules (pieces, flavors, helpers, quips, Pal names, outfits, decor, Glowbugs, event themes) and is translated by id with English as fallback (`src/i18n/gameText.js`, `palText.js`, `groveText.js`, `shopText.js`, `extrasText.js`); content-only key prefixes are listed in the translations test. English error messages from game modules and the server are shown translated through `errorText` (`src/i18n/meText.js`) when known. Outside English, levels are titled by their number ("Nivel 148"/"שלב 148") and worlds "Mundo 1"/"עולם 1" (the English names are wordplay). The language follows the device until chosen in Me → Language (`settings.language`). Hebrew sets `dir="rtl"` on the page and uses the Rubik font (Fredoka's Hebrew letters ך and ג were misread); the puzzle board stays `dir="ltr"` (light directions are absolute) and back/play/skip icons flip. New user-facing text goes through `t()`.
- **Loading** (`src/components/Skeleton.jsx`): content that loads from the server shows soft placeholder blocks in its real shape (slow shimmer, still for reduced motion, `aria-busy` on the region), never a spinner or a wrong default like "Pick a nickname". Shared data keeps the last loaded copy so a page opened again shows it at once while it refreshes (`useWeeklyBoard`).
- **Saved progress** (`src/lib/keepProgress.js`): a guest's progress lives only on the device, so the game asks the browser to keep its storage (`navigator.storage.persist`); best effort, Safari may still clear a site left unvisited for a week unless it's on the Home Screen.
- **Narrow phones** (pre-release test): screens must fit 320px wide with no sideways scroll; rules for it sit under `@media (max-width: 360px)` (Shop shelf, helper row with a smaller Lumi, Pals tabs without icons and a 2-column collection). Dialogs focus their button without scrolling, so a tall one opens at its title; the level card keeps Tab on itself.
