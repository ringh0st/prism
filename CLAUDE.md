# Lumipals: project guide

Lumipals is a light-bending puzzle game: rotate mirrors and crystals to steer beams of light so every gem lights up. It is for all ages and is published at https://lumipals.app/ (GitHub Pages with a custom domain; the domain is at Cloudflare, DNS only; the old https://ringh0st.github.io/prism/ redirects there). The game is at `/play/`, the home page at `/`. The store name is "Rinny's Lumipals: Light Puzzle". It was called Prism before; the repository and `prism.*` storage keys keep that name on purpose, so players keep their progress.

## Product requirements (from the owner, apply to all work)

- **Best UX/UI:** polished, delightful, minimal and fun. Every interaction gets clear, juicy feedback.
- **Colors:** warm, relaxing and calm (owner feedback: bright yellow and red felt too fiery). **Purple and blue must not be the main colors.** Blue may appear only as a small in-game color (for example one beam color among several), never as the brand, UI or background color.
- **Addictive, dopamine-rich loop:** satisfying solve moments, stars, streaks, rewards, unlocks and progress you can see.
- **Levels with a difficulty rhythm:** mostly approachable levels, with a **Hard** level now and then and an occasional **Extra Hard** challenge level. These are clearly marked and give bigger rewards.
- **Challenging and game-like, not a web page:** 1000+ levels (50 worlds × 20, `npm run levels`), move limits, an adventure map, chunky outlined game UI, a mascot (Lumi) with funny lines, and surprises (disco/night/boss levels, chests).
- **Rewards:** 1–3 stars per level based on moves, world completion rewards, a daily puzzle with a streak, and cosmetic unlocks.
- **Accounts:** play as a guest immediately with local progress. Signing in with Google or Apple is optional and syncs progress, using Supabase. The Apple button always shows in the iPhone app and appears on the web once the Apple provider is enabled in Supabase (`src/lib/authProviders.js`).
- **Admin:** only the owner can use a level editor and publish levels, and has a testing switch in Me → account that opens every level on that device without writing fake progress (`src/lib/openAllLevels.js`). Levels are data in the database, so new levels appear without an app update. Its Stats tab shows anonymous play counts (`supabase/play_stats.sql`, `usePlayStats`, `src/game/playCounts.js`): tries, win rate, out-of-moves and gave-up per level, guests included, no personal data, and a Walls list of levels most players fail.
- **Responsive and accessible:** phone-first, and works on tablet and desktop too. Full keyboard support, screen-reader labels, visible focus, good color contrast, reduced-motion support, and color is never the only signal (use shapes and symbols too).
- **Code quality:** best practices, reusable components and modules, consistent naming, small focused files, no dead code.

## Economy (see src/game/catalog.js and supabase/economy.sql)

- **Sparks** are earned by playing. **Diamonds** are premium: earned by finishing a world, 3-starring a world or every 7-day streak, or bought with real money on the website (Stripe Managed Payments as merchant of record; Lemon Squeezy until it closes on 31 March 2027; the `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER` secret picks one, see PAYMENTS.md). Diamonds always require an account.
- **Helpers:** Hint, Lens, Auto-turn and Skip. They are capped so buying isn't a shortcut through the game: max 5 held per helper, 3 helpers bought with sparks per day, and star caps (Hint/Lens → 2★, Auto-turn/Skip → 1★). Sparks can never become diamonds.
- For signed-in players the **server is the source of truth**: every currency change goes through a `security definer` function in `supabase/economy.sql`, never a direct table write. Prices exist in both `catalog.js` and `economy.sql`; a test keeps them in sync.
- **Boosters** (`BOOSTERS` in catalog.js, `useBoosters`, `BoosterPicker`): three pre-level boosters picked on the level card (main road from World 2; not in the daily, events, Rush or challenges) and spent at Let's go: **Spare Moves** (+3 to the move limit, stars unchanged), **Undo** (`steadyHand`: take back up to 2 taps; the tap still counts for stars but the move comes back; the button sits in the header) and **Head Start** (Lumi turns one mirror of the best solution before the start, not a move, ★★ at most, kept through restarts). Held like helpers (same `helpers` inventory, cap 5, server `use_helper`), bought only with sparks (60) inside the 3-a-day limit, never diamonds. They also come from calendar days 3 and 5 and from presents' helper surprise. Touching a booster keeps the card up until Let's go.
- **Win streak** (src/game/winStreak.js): main-road levels won in a row (`progress.winStreak`, on this device). From 3 in a row every level starts with a free Spare Moves, from 6 also a free Undo (shown on the level card with a flame and the streak; nothing is taken from the inventory). Running out of moves ends it; leaving, restarting or a Skip doesn't.
- **Level numbers and milestones** (`levelNumber` in worlds.js, src/game/milestones.js): built-in levels are numbered 1–1000 on the map, PLAY and titles ("Level 148", the world as the subtitle); ids stay `w8-08`. Solving the 50th, 100th, 250th, 500th and 1000th level throws a party on the win screen (trophy with the number, big celebration).
- **Pals** (profile characters, src/game/pals.js and supabase/pals.sql): 48 Pals, including 8 Legendary and 10 Mythic ones from myths and legends (phoenix, qilin, griffin…). Every Pal is a hand-drawn kawaii sticker with one SVG per form (`src/assets/pals/drawn/<id>-<1…5>.svg`, Baby to Master, drawn by `PalArt`) on a shared 100×100 stage: head top y≈25, eyes (39,51)/(61,51), neck y≈70, floor y≈94, so every outfit piece fits every Pal and form (capes and cloaks, `Cloak`, hang behind it with a collar and clasp in front; worn items are drawn like the Pals, a thin line in a deeper shade of their own colors plus a soft shadow (`ItemOutline` in PalArt), so they stand out on Pals of the same color; hats sit deep on the head, brim on the forehead (`HAT_DEPTH`), fitted to each drawing, widened to its forehead and lowered onto heads that sit low, from `palOutfitFit.json`, which `npm run pals:fit` measures). Each form is drawn older: signature features grow (tail, mane, wings, antlers, shell), Champ adds a small new detail and Master a crown-like feature of its own with a golden glow; every form is shown at the same size (growing up shows in how it looks, never in size: each drawing has its own scale in `palFit.json`; run `npm run pals:fit` after adding or redrawing Pals). The first Pal turns up as a surprise reward after 5 solved levels ("You found a friend!", `src/game/firstFriend.js`, `FriendFoundDialog`), so the game reads as a puzzle game first; unlock the rest by playing. Only the partner Pal grows: every solved level (not a Skip) adds one, and 20 grow it into its next form (Baby, Junior, Buddy, Champ, Master); each Pal keeps its own count. Evolution can never be bought; sparks only buy cosmetic accessories. **Pal powers** (src/game/palPowers.js, `PalPowerBadge`, `PalPowerChip`): while it's the partner, every Pal helps in main-road levels with one power (`power` in pals.js): extra moves, take-backs or free hints. It grows with the form (Baby/Junior 1, Buddy/Champ 2, Master 3; Legendary and Mythic start one step stronger) and never lowers the ★★★ target (moves and take-backs only add to the limit; a free hint caps at ★★ like any hint). Not in the daily, events, Rush or challenges. It's shown as a coin with the amount on Pal cards, team tiles, the forms strip, the partner hero, the first-friend card and the level card (playtesters didn't know what Pals were for). The Pals screen's Collection tab (`PalTeam`) lists every Pal; "Play with" (`TeamSwitcher`, `playablePals`) holds every owned or unlocked Pal (an unlocked one joins the moment it's picked, no separate "add" step), and the level card's Pal chip opens the same switcher, so the partner (and its power) can be changed right before a level. **How Pals work** (`PalGuide`): one picture loop (win → it grows, bigger → stronger help, 10 ★ → a present, more Pals join and you pick who plays), opened by the ? on the partner hero and by itself on the first Pals visit when no other Pal notice is up; in levels a first-time spotlight points at the helping Pal (`pal` in levelTips.js), and the win screen shows the stronger power when a Pal grows.
- **The Grove** (src/game/grove.js, supabase/grove.sql): the Pals' home in the enchanted forest (Pals → Grove → Decorate); only the partner Pal is shown there. 22 decor items in 7 spots (backdrop, home, seat, light, garden, water, sky), bought with sparks and placed one per spot; a **background** (`backdrop`: Blossom hills, Snowy pines, Desert oasis, Sunny cove, `groveBackdrops.jsx`) swaps the forest for its own land under the same time-of-day sky (no falling leaves in the desert or at the beach). The Pal there is alive (`src/game/groveLife.js`, `usePalLife`): each Pal there (`GrovePal`) wanders, plays (jump, spin, dance…), uses placed decor (sits on the seat, splashes in the water…), follows the player's time of day (dawn/day/dusk/night sky) and naps 23:00–06:00; tapping pets it. The forest changes with the seasons (`seasonOf`: snow, petals, autumn leaves), one of your other Pals visits most daytimes (`visitingFriend`), and finishing a world throws a Grove party once (`useGroveParty`). After each win the partner cheers (`src/game/palCheers.js`). Once a day the partner says hello on the map beside PLAY (`palGreetings.js`, `PalHello`): news first (almost grown, event weekend, streak), otherwise a time-of-day hello; it waits for the splash (`useIsSplashShowing`).
- **Gifts** (src/game/gifts.js, supabase/gifts.sql, `src/components/gifts/`): every gift opens the same way (`GiftReveal`: a wrapped box or chest waits for a tap, wobbles, pops open, and the things inside come out one by one with a chime; a tap shows everything; calm for reduced motion). The daily gift is a **7-day gift calendar** (`GIFT_CALENDAR`, `GiftCalendar`): it grows through the week, day 7 is the big gift (a diamond for accounts, a Skip for guests), and a missed day pauses it (it never resets). The partner Pal brings a **present** for every 10 ★ from built-in levels (`PresentButton` on the map bar with a filling meter, `PalPresent` beside PLAY holding it, `PresentNote` on the win screen): 20 sparks plus one surprise with its odds shown (`PresentOdds`: helper 45%, a bag of 40 more sparks 30%, an outfit piece or Grove decor you don't own 25%), drawn on the server for accounts (`claim_present`). Inspired by Brawl Stars' Starr Drops (research: the surprise reveal brought its players back), each present also has a rarity decided when it's drawn and revealed as it opens: it glows up step by step (`GiftReveal` glow steps, color plus a symbol ◆ ★ ✦, `sfx.glowUp`): Plain 65%, Rare 23% (+20 sparks), Epic 9% (+40, a sure item), Legendary 3% (+80, a sure item, a diamond for accounts or 40 sparks for guests) (`PRESENT_RARITIES`, odds shown in `PresentOdds`). No dud results: the plainest present still holds sparks plus a helper, a sparks bag or an item. Several waiting presents open together in one window, one after another, then a summary (`GiftReveal` `count`); each present charges up first (a bar fills notch by notch ◆ ★ ✦, glowing up at each notch its rarity reaches), and a tap hurries it along. Presents start once the first Pal is found. **Win gifts** (`WIN_GIFTS`, `WinGiftLadder` on the win screen, `claim_win_gift`): the 1st, 3rd and 6th different level won each day (not a Skip) bring a small gift (10 sparks; 15 + Hint; 25 + Lens); the server counts its own results since the player's midnight; a day without play costs nothing. Run gifts.sql after rush.sql (and again after re-running social.sql, pals.sql or grove.sql).
- **Pal records** (src/game/palRecords.js, `PalRecords`, `PalRecordNote`): five medals per Pal earned together while it's the partner (5 Hard wins, ★★★ in 10 levels, 3 gems in one tap, a boss with ★★★, growing into a Master). A row of medals with filling rings on the Pal's card (words only on tap) and a shining medal on the win screen. Pride only: they pay nothing, can't be bought, and are counted on this device.
- **Show, don't tell** (owner feedback, like Brawl Stars): rarity and rewards read from color, light, size, symbols and sound, not words. The present's rarity is a color, a ring of light and a big symbol (words only for screen readers); the odds are pictures with percentages. Prefer icons and motion over sentences in new UI.
- **Nicknames** (src/game/nicknames.js, supabase/nicknames.sql): players type their Top-board name or roll a random one. The same rules run in the game and on the server: 3–16 letters/numbers/spaces, no long digit runs, and a blocked-word list (kept in sync by a test).
- **Titles and player level** (src/game/titles.js): titles are earned by stars, each with its own color via TitleBadge, which also shows the player's Glow (★ and a number) (`playerLevelFor`: Lv 2 at 5 ★, rising slower later, Lv 35 at 2975 ★). The Me screen shows the level bar and the Title road (`TitleRoad`); the win screen shows Level up!/New title! (`climbFor`, `LevelUp`).
- **Player cards** (supabase/player_cards.sql, `PlayerCardDialog`): tap anyone on the Top board to see their partner Pal, title, weekly points, stars, worlds and Pals collected, each next to yours. Looked up by board place (no account ids are exposed). The Me screen shows Pals collected too.
- **Rarity and the Shop:** Pals are Basic, ★ Legendary (8, only from Lucky gifts or the Shop) or ✦ Mythic (10). The Shop shows 6 Basic Pals a week per player (`weeklyAdoptShelf`/`_weekly_basics`, new ones every Monday UTC, owned ones stay marked) to adopt by choice (20💎); each day every player gets one random Legendary (40💎) and one random Mythic (60💎) pick, always shown before buying, and can adopt at most 2 Legendary/Mythic Pals a week (`RARE_ADOPTS_PER_WEEK`, enforced in `adopt_pal`). In Hebrew the rarities are רגיל, אגדי and מיתולוגי. **Lucky Pal gift:** every 200 solved levels a free random Pal you don't have (70% Basic, 20% Legendary, 10% Mythic, odds shown), drawn on the server for accounts (`claim_lucky_pal`).
- **Weekend events** (src/game/events.js, supabase/events.sql, `npm run events`): every Saturday and Sunday 10 themed puzzles (themes take turns: Mirror Madness, Portal Party…), made ahead of time in src/data/events/ (one file per weekend, a year ahead; run `npm run events` to add more). Open after World 1; no Skip; each solve grows the partner Pal. Finishing all 10 pays 150 sparks and that event's stamp (collected on the event page).
- **Light Rush** (src/game/rush.js, supabase/rush.sql, `src/screens/rush/`, `useRushRun`): a weekly run (Monday, UTC) of 30 built-in puzzles, two from each of Worlds 1–15, the same for everyone. Opens after World 3. One pot of 12 spare moves for the whole run: solving in par costs nothing, extra moves and restarts spend spares (the run is saved on every move, so leaving mid-puzzle counts as a restart), +3 every 5 puzzles; running out ends the run. No helpers. Milestones (5/10/20/30 puzzles) pay sparks once a week; a test keeps them in sync with rush.sql. Weekly board of best runs under nicknames (`rush_board`).
- **Pal quests** (src/game/quests.js, supabase/quests.sql): each week (Monday, UTC) the partner Pal asks for three goals (solve levels, ★★★ levels, Hard levels; the bigger or smaller of each takes turns), counted once per level per week and paid in sparks. The server counts from its own level results; a test keeps the quest list in sync.
- **Daily missions** (src/game/dailyMissions.js, supabase/daily_missions.sql, `DailyMissions`, `MissionsButton`): three small goals a day from the player's local midnight (like win gifts): one "solve N" plus two of other kinds (★★★, Hard, daily puzzle, Glowbug rescue; guests also combo and present), drawn the same way for everyone from the missions the player can already do (`minSolved`, from 3 solved levels) and kept once the day starts. 10–20 sparks each, +10 with the last of the three. Shown as "Today" rings above the Pal quests (Pals → Quests, opened through `GiftReveal`); a tick on the map bar while one waits. Accounts: the server keeps the day's three and counts from its own results (`my_daily_missions`, `claim_daily_mission`; guests-only missions are left out); a test keeps both in sync. Run daily_missions.sql after gifts.sql and glowbugs.sql.
- **Bonus goals and personal bests** (src/game/bonusGoals.js, supabase/bonus_goals.sql, `useBonusGoal`, `BonusGoalBadge`): a reason to replay after ★★★. Every main-road level from 1-4 has one optional goal about *how* it's solved (★★★ already means fewest taps and no helpers): 2 gems in 1 tap, a marked gem lit first or last, only turn lit pieces, leave a marked piece alone, burn every shadow (No helpers only as a last resort). It's seeded by the level id and picked only from goals the board allows within the move limit, preferring ones some ★★★ order misses; each comes with a witness (taps that solve and meet it) that a test replays for all 1000 levels. Worked out in a Web Worker (`findBonusGoal`), shown on the level card (icon + few words), as a chip beside the gem counter (tick when done, cross when missed, tap for Lumi to say it), as a tag on the marked gem or piece, and on the win screen. The first time pays 10 sparks (guests locally; accounts through `claim_bonus_goals`, which pays only solved built-in levels once and returns the list; sync sends the device's list so guest/offline goals get paid). Stored as `progress.bonusGoals`; map nodes show a small mint tick. Not in the daily, events, Rush or challenges. The level card shows the best moves so far (trophy + number) and the win screen a beaten best as trophy, old number crossed out → new. Run bonus_goals.sql after economy.sql.
- **Weekly theme collections** (src/game/themes.js, supabase/themed_shop.sql): each week (Monday, UTC) the Shop's Pal section opens on "This week": one theme's outfit pieces and Grove decor (Elven Woods, Autumn Harvest, Winter Wonderland, Pirate Cove take turns), some for sparks and the showpieces for diamonds. Every collection comes back later in the year (no "now or never"). Art: `themeOutfitArt.jsx`, `themeDecorShapes.jsx`. Re-run themed_shop.sql after re-running pals.sql or grove.sql (it updates wear/place for theme items).
- **Try it on** (`TryOnDialog`): tapping any outfit piece or decoration in the Shop shows it on your own Pal (or in your Grove, without the Pals so nothing hides it) first, then Buy → "Wear it now"/"Place it now". Short on sparks → Play; short on diamonds → the Diamonds tab (ask-a-parent note; earn them in the app); diamonds need an account.
- All-ages fairness: **no paid loot boxes**: anything bought is shown before paying. Random rewards are only free, earned gifts with their odds shown. No fake timers or pressure tactics, clear prices, and an "ask a parent" note wherever money is involved.

## Conventions

- React + Vite, plain CSS with design tokens (CSS custom properties) defined once and themed for light and dark.
- Components: `PascalCase.jsx`, one component per file, colocated `ComponentName.module.css`.
- Hooks: `useSomething.js`. Pure game logic (beam tracing, level validation, scoring) lives in `src/game/` with no React or DOM, so it can be unit tested.
- Names describe intent: `traceBeam`, `isLevelSolved`, `starsForMoves`, not `doStuff` or `helper`.
- Sound and haptics live in `src/audio/` (generated music-box tones, no files). Each moment has its own named sound (`sfx.outOfMoves`, `sfx.palGrow`…) and a haptic strength; keep failure sounds kind, never harsh. Inside a world, the level-start jingle, gem chimes and win fanfare use that world's voice (`src/audio/worldVoices.js`: scale, instrument, motif and a nature sound; set by `sfx.setWorldVoice`). iPhone haptics use the hidden-switch trick in `haptics.js`. Levels have quiet generated background music in the world's voice (`src/audio/music.js`: soft pad plus sparse wandering notes, never the same twice; fades out for the win, stops when the app is hidden), with its own Music switch in Settings. Sound effects and music share one context (`audioContext.js`).
- **Pieces and twists** (`src/game/pieces.js`): a new piece every world through world 17 (the last five are the darkness), taught one per level with a spotlight on the board, never two introductions in a row (a test walks worlds 1–15): mirrors 1-1, walls 1-4, fixed mirrors 1-10, splitters 2-1, color glass 3-1, shadow crystals 4-1, **twin mirrors** 5-1 (same mark = turn together; `link`), **portals** 6-1 (same pair = light jumps across; `pair`), **cracking mirrors** 7-1 (`fragile`: 3 turns, then fixed; hints treat cracked ones as locked), **fog** 8-1 (`fog` on gems/walls/shades: hidden until light touches the cell), **prism crystals** 9-1 (fixed `prism` tile: light splits out to both sides), **light gates** 10-1 (`gate` + `switch` with the same `pair`: the gate is a wall until light hits its switch; `traceBeams` retraces until the open gates settle; the first lamp's light feeds the switch, so gate levels have two lamps), **turning lamps** 11-1 (emitter with `turnable`: tap swings it a quarter turn clockwise and back, `lampDirection`), **one-sided mirrors** 12-1 (`oneSided: 0 | 1`, the silvered face; the dark back blocks light, `hitsSilveredFace`). Later worlds mix them in (`TWIST_WORLDS`/`TWIST_MIX` in worlds.js). **The darkness** (worlds 13–17, `src/game/darkness.js`): Smudge (כתמון), a cheeky shadow, steals the light, and his pieces race the player move by move, so the order of taps matters: **creeping shadows** 13-1 (`level.glooms: [{ trail: [cells], gem }]`: a cell per move toward the gem, light burns it where it stands or as it creeps into light, past its trail it swallows the gem), **fading gems** 14-1 (`fade: n`: light it within n moves or it goes dark), **dying lamps** 15-1 (`power`, `reach`: the light reaches `reach` + power left cells; out of power, the lamp goes out), **voids** 16-1 (fixed `void` tile: swallows light in its cell and the four next to it, in `traceBeams`) and **eclipses** 17-1 (`level.eclipse: 3`: every 3rd move the darkness moves twice). The game replays the standing taps (`replayDarkness` in `usePuzzle`, so an Undo rolls the darkness back), the board draws it (`DarknessLayer`, `darkTileArt.jsx`: Smudge gliding over his dotted trail, countdown badges, the void's halo, the eclipse chip), losing to it ends the level like running out of moves with its own headline (`out.dark.*`; in Light Rush it's a restart), and hints pick a tap in a safe order (`findSafeOrder`). The builder adds it on top of a finished board (`darknessBuilder.js`, `darknessFor`/`DARK_WORLDS`/`DARK_MIX` in worlds.js): voids where they cut off cheaper routes, then lamp power, fade deadlines and shadow trails tuned so a safe order exists but random orders often lose (`orderRisk`; gentler on normal levels, and the piece's first level gives plenty of room). From world 15 bosses bring Smudge's shadows (two from world 25) and show his face; Lumi introduces him on 13-1 (`intro.smudge`). Every new piece keeps two states per switch, so the solver still finds par exactly; the solver treats twins as one switch. Difficulty follows the level number 1–1000 (`generatorPresetFor`: moves needed rise from about 4 to 13 by level 500 and 20 at the end; the light path and decoys grow with them), so a new world never starts easier than the one before. The solver follows the light and only decides pieces the light reaches (`NeedsDecision` in beam.js), so boards can have up to 30 turning pieces; solutions are `{ mask, care }` bit masks. After changing `generatorPresetFor`, run `npm run levels -- <worlds>`.
- Level data is versioned JSON validated by one schema shared by the game and the editor.
- **First steps** (`isFirstStepsLevel` in PlayScreen, `CoachHand`): levels 1-1 to 1-3 teach on the board itself (a pointing hand on the next mirror to turn; in 1-2 and 1-3 only after a few seconds without a tap, Lamp/Gem name tags, Lumi explaining) instead of a "How to play" card, hide the helpers and star markers, and wait for a tap before starting. A brand-new player goes straight from the splash into 1-1 (`FirstLaunch`); the map's extras (daily, gift, events, Light Rush, install) appear after 3 solved levels.
- **Welcome tour and spotlights** (`src/screens/welcome/`, `src/components/tour/Spotlight.jsx`, `src/game/levelTips.js`): the 4-slide picture tour (tiny real boards that play themselves) is under Me → How to play, not before the first level (playtest research: docs/playtest-research.md). In levels, new pieces and first-time tips (stars, helpers) are taught by dimming the screen and highlighting the real thing on the board with one short line and "Got it!" (no text cards). Piece texts stay a few words. After 30 s without a move, or 6 taps that light nothing new, the hint ladder starts for free: a soft glow on the area to look at, Lumi's nudge (with her sing-song voice, `sfx.lumiTalk`) and a wiggling Hint button (Hint marks the mirror, Auto-turn turns it). A Calm board setting stills the scenery and the Pal. The meter shows moves left, the best stars still possible (moves so far plus the fewest taps left, `tapsLeft` in solver.js, so a star drops the moment it's out of reach) and lit gems (1/2); the first steps show no meter at all. The intro shows the goal as "★★★ in N moves" and a HARD LEVEL stamp on Hard levels. Tapping a piece that can't turn shakes it and Lumi says why. World 1 offers only Hint, and in Worlds 1–2 the first Hint in each level is free; using a helper, Lumi says the best result left ("Best now: ★★"). The board is sized from the measured free height (`useAvailableHeight`) and the partner Pal watches from the header corner. The player's star level is called **Glow** and shown as ★ plus a number (never "level", which means a puzzle, or "rank", which means a place on the Top board). In Worlds 1–2, running out of moves offers **Keep going** (+5 moves, 1★ at most) instead of a fail. The win screen puts Next right under the result; extras (Pal, finds, sharing) follow below. Say "Get ★★★ in N moves", never "par".
- **Level flavors** (`src/game/recipes.js`): in Worlds 1–3 almost every level has a flavor (Two lamps, Light show, One tap wonder, Long road, Tiny but tricky, Wall maze, Gem shower, Rainbow), stored as `recipe` in the level data and shown with one of its goal lines (they vary) on the intro and by Lumi. Level names never repeat within a world (`levelNameFor`, a test checks). No two in a row, and 5-level arcs: a Hard level (5, 10, 15) is followed by a quick breather (one tap wonder or tiny board); a test checks both. Playtesters found the game too easy, so regular flavors need two more moves than their base (levels 11+ one more again), spare moves are tight (`moveLimitFor`: par + a third, Hard + a quarter, Extra Hard + 15%, at least 1), and regular levels in Worlds 1–6 must have a catch: random tapping may solve at most 8% (`src/game/levelQuality.js`, checked by the level builder; tutorials, piece introductions and breathers are exempt). Piece introductions, tutorials and bosses stay plain. After changing them, run `npm run levels -- 1 2 3`.
- **Juice** (playtest and research: docs in the commit history): lighting a gem is an event (`gemBurst` with a ring of light, then `flyToCounter` sends a spark to that gem's slot in the counter, which bounces, with a rising `sfx.gemCollect`); every turn has a crisp click; a star slipping out of reach wobbles and drops with `sfx.starLost`; after a win every move left over bursts along the winning light path (`src/lib/lightFinale.js`, `sfx.finaleBurst`, Lumi: "2 moves to spare!"; celebration only, nothing paid) before the result, whose sparks count up (`useCountUp`, `sfx.countTick`); Hard and boss wins get their own headline and the big celebration.
- **In-level fun** (`combos.js`, `ComboBurst`, `PalBuddy`): one tap lighting 2+ gems bursts "Double!/Triple!/MEGA!" with a rising chime; the partner Pal watches beside the board in its outfit and reacts (hop, spin, shake, shiver, dance, droop).
- **Glowbugs** (src/game/glowbugs.js, `GlowbugArt`, `GlowbugGarden`): 60 critters (12 kinds × 5 colors, names and bios) nap on ~1 in 4 built-in levels and every boss (rare kinds on Hard/boss), on an empty cell of the solution's light path (on the lamp if none). They wake in the light; ★★★ rescues them. Rescues are derived from stars (`rescuedGlowbugs`), so nothing extra is saved. Map nodes show waiting Glowbugs; the Garden is at #/glowbugs (link in Pals → Collection). The Garden shows one row per kind (its 5 colors); a full row pays 1 diamond once for accounts, shown at the row's end as a wrapped gift with a diamond tag and a dot per color (it wiggles in a golden glow when full and opens through `GiftReveal`) (`GlowbugRowPrize`, `claim_glowbug_row` in supabase/glowbugs.sql, whose `glowbug_levels` table a test keeps in sync with `glowbugForLevel`; run it after gifts.sql). **How Glowbugs work** (`GlowbugGuide`, built on the shared `PictureGuide`, `GuideButton` and `useFirstTimeGuide` like `PalGuide`): nap → light wakes it, ★★★ → home to the Garden, 5 colors → a diamond, a sleeping bug on the map marks one; opened by the Garden's ? and by itself on the first visit, and the first level with a Glowbug points at its cell (`glowbug` in levelTips.js). In Hebrew they're זוהרונים.
- **Treats and stickers** (src/game/treats.js, src/game/stickers.js): every 3rd solved level the partner finds a treat (fed on the Pals screen for a trick; only `treatsFed` is stored). Six stickers per world (Explorer, Starry, Hard Hero, Boss Beater, Bug Buddy, Perfect!) come from stars; the album is at #/stickers (link on the Me screen). New treats and stickers show on the win screen.
- **3D board** (`src/components/board3d/`: `Board3D`, `boardScene.js`, `boardPieces.js`; three.js, loaded only when used): the puzzle board is a 3D tabletop in the flat board's own world colors (tray, rounded tiles that warm to honey under light, every piece as a chunky toy with soft shadows; only light glows). Drag turns it all the way round and tilts it, pinch or the wheel zooms, small buttons turn, zoom and reset; at zoom 1 the whole table always fits. Taps are found by casting a ray into the scene (a drag is never a tap); the board's buttons stay, placed over the pieces, for the keyboard, screen readers, hint rings, the coach hand, Smudge and countdowns. On by default where WebGL works; Settings → Feel has a "3D board" switch back to the flat board (`settings.board3d`). Only the board is 3D: everything around it is unchanged.
- **Level landscapes** (`src/screens/play/WorldBackdrop.jsx`, `backdrop/`): each level gets its own arrangement of the world's scenery plus two of the world's animals (`BIOME_CREATURES`: camels, lizards and tumbleweeds in the desert, ducks at the lagoon…), seeded by level id. Keep them small, slow and quiet; nothing moves for reduced motion.
- **Challenge links** (`src/game/challenges.js`, `ChallengeShare`, `ChallengeRoute`, `ChallengeResultDialog`): the win screen's "Challenge a friend" shares `lumipals.app/play/#/challenge/<level>/<moves>/<nickname>` (public nickname only, checked by the nickname rules). Anyone, even a new guest, plays that level in `mode="challenge"`: no helpers, nothing saved, then sees both scores and can challenge back. Everything is in the link; no database.
- **Solve replay** (`src/game/replay.js`, `src/lib/replay/`, `ReplayShare`): the win screen's "Share replay" records the winning taps as an 8–15 s vertical MP4 (canvas + MediaRecorder, drawn in the game's light style, ending with the stars and lumipals.app) and opens the share sheet, or downloads it. Hidden where the browser can't record.
- **Splash and version** (`src/components/splash/SplashScreen.jsx`, `src/lib/appInfo.js`): a 4-second opening screen with the game name, "A Ringhost's Games production" and the version from `package.json` (bump it for each release). Tap, Enter or Escape skips it; pop-ups wait until it's gone.
- **iPhone app** (IOS.md): Capacitor wraps the built game (`ios/`, bundle id `app.lumipals`, `npm run ios`). Use `isNativeApp` from `src/lib/platform.js` for app-only behavior: no web purchases or auto-update in the app, native haptics, sign-in via a Safari sheet returning to `app.lumipals://auth`, and Sign in with Apple.
- **Languages** (`src/i18n/`): English, Spanish and Hebrew. `useI18n()` gives `t(key, params, fallback)` (plurals via `count` and `key.one`/`key.other`, `{name}` placeholders, English fallback) and `line(key, englishLines)` for Lumi's quips. Dictionaries: `src/i18n/strings/{en,es,he}.js` (play, map, win) plus one file per area in `src/i18n/strings/sections/` (me, pals, shop, grove, extras; each exports en/es/he); English is the source, and tests check that every key the code uses exists and that es/he match en's keys and placeholders. Game content keeps its English in the game modules (pieces, flavors, helpers, quips, Pal names, outfits, decor, Glowbugs, event themes) and is translated by id with English as fallback (`src/i18n/gameText.js`, `palText.js`, `groveText.js`, `shopText.js`, `extrasText.js`); content-only key prefixes are listed in the translations test. English error messages from game modules and the server are shown translated through `errorText` (`src/i18n/meText.js`) when known. Outside English, levels are titled by their number ("Nivel 148"/"שלב 148") and worlds "Mundo 1"/"עולם 1" (the English names are wordplay). The language follows the device until chosen in Me → Language (`settings.language`). Hebrew sets `dir="rtl"` on the page and uses the Rubik font (Fredoka's Hebrew letters ך and ג were misread); the puzzle board stays `dir="ltr"` (light directions are absolute) and back/play/skip icons flip. New user-facing text goes through `t()`.
- **Loading** (`src/components/Skeleton.jsx`): content that loads from the server shows soft placeholder blocks in its real shape (slow shimmer, still for reduced motion, `aria-busy` on the region), never a spinner or a wrong default like "Pick a nickname". Shared data keeps the last loaded copy so a page opened again shows it at once while it refreshes (`useWeeklyBoard`).
